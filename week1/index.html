Relationships are part of the explanation

An individual perspective asks what characteristics a person has. A network perspective also asks who connects to whom, and how these connections affect information, support, and opportunities.

A network is more than a social media platform

Nodes represent actors such as students, organizations, or cities. Ties represent a specified relationship such as collaboration, advice, or communication. Social networks also exist offline.

Among the same students, friendship and advice seeking can produce different networks. A follow can be one-way; joint authorship is shared. A tie can record whether contact occurred or how often it occurred.

Five students, five collaboration ties. A line means two students collaborated.

Define the actors, ties, and time window

Who is included?

For example, all GE2234 students this semester, including those with no recorded ties.

What is a tie?

For example, asking another student for course advice at least once.

When?

Specify a window, such as the past two weeks. Relationships change over time.

Whose network?

An ego network centers on one person and their contacts. A whole network records ties among all actors within a defined boundary.

Missing data matters

No recorded tie does not always mean no relationship exists. Someone may not respond, or the source may capture only one type of interaction.

Practice thinking in relationships

A survey asks: “Whom did you ask for course advice in the past two weeks?” What does an arrow from A to B mean?

Return to the collaboration diagram

If C–D disappears, can E still reach A through collaboration ties?

Show explanation

No. A, B, and C remain connected, while D and E form a separate component. C–D is the only connection between these groups. A single tie can matter for the whole network.

Discuss

Choose a setting you know. Define its nodes, ties, boundary, and time window. What question could this network help answer?

Interpret carefully

Being well connected does not imply influence in every situation. A network pattern alone does not establish causation: similarity may reflect selection, shared circumstances, or influence.
